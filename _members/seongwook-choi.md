## Biography

Seongwook Choi is an Assistant Professor in the **School of Health and Environmental Science at Korea University** and the Principal Investigator of the **Multimodal Acoustic Imaging Laboratory (MAI Lab)**. His research develops acoustic imaging technologies that connect **photoacoustic imaging, ultrasound imaging, radiation-induced acoustics, quantitative reconstruction, and artificial intelligence**. He is particularly interested in building complete imaging pipelines - from hardware and signal acquisition to image reconstruction, quantitative analysis, and biomedical validation. His broader goal is to engineer imaging and acoustic-stimulation protocols that provide repeatable measures of disease progression, physiological dynamics, and biological responses to treatment, neuromodulation, and rehabilitation.

Before joining Korea University, he was a Postdoctoral Scholar in the **Department of Radiology, School of Medicine at Stanford University**, where he worked with Prof. Katherine W. Ferrara on ultrasound/photoacoustic imaging and deep-learning-based biomedical image analysis. He previously conducted postdoctoral research at the **Institute of Artificial Intelligence at POSTECH** and served as a visiting researcher in the **Department of Nuclear Medicine at Seoul National University**.

He received his Ph.D. in Convergence IT Engineering from POSTECH under the supervision of Prof. Chulhong Kim. His doctoral dissertation, *Photon-induced Acoustic Imaging: from Hardware Implementation to Deep Learning Application*, integrated imaging-system development, 3D photoacoustic computed tomography, X-ray-induced acoustic imaging, GPU-based reconstruction, and deep-learning-enhanced volumetric imaging. His undergraduate training was in Mechanical Engineering at POSTECH, providing the engineering foundation for his current interdisciplinary work across imaging physics, instrumentation, computation, and medicine.

## Research Interests

- Multimodal acoustic and biomedical imaging
- Photoacoustic computed tomography and ultrasound imaging
- Radiation-induced acoustic imaging, X-ray acoustics, and dosimetry
- AI-enhanced reconstruction, segmentation, generation, and quantitative imaging
- 3D imaging systems, signal processing, inverse problems, and GPU-accelerated computation
- Preclinical and translational applications for disease and treatment monitoring, ultrasound stimulation, neuromodulation, and rehabilitation assessment
